# FAC-307: privacy policy with the Limited Use statement (grilling decisions)

**Status: pending owner review (2026-11-26).** Ran together with FAC-175 (scope and legal applicability: [FAC-175.md](FAC-175.md)). Process: [FAC-307-process.md](FAC-307-process.md). Not legal advice; **[LAWYER]** marks what should be reviewed before Public. Sources read 2026-11-26.

## Decisions

1. **Where it lives: `https://linklog.facundopri.com/privacy`.** A static, script-free `src/privacy.html` copied into `dist/` by one line at the end of `scripts/build.ts` (`await Bun.write("dist/privacy.html", Bun.file("src/privacy.html"))`). The Worker's assets serve it before the SPA fallback.
   - Proven by running (both debaters, separate worktrees): after `bun run build`, `wrangler dev` with the real `assets` block answers `/privacy` → 200 with the policy title and no cookie; `/privacy.html` and `/privacy/` → 307 to `/privacy`; `/anything-else` → the SPA. `index.html` and the app chunks are byte-identical to the baseline (`shasum`), the page has 0 `<script>` and 0 `googleapis`, and `tests/bundle.test.ts` and the private-path check pass.
   - Why this URL: the app host is the only Linklog domain with a custom domain and HTTPS today; the landing is on `workers.dev` and `/privacy` there is 404. Today `https://linklog.facundopri.com/privacy` returns 200 **with the SPA** (fallback), so after deploy verify `curl -s https://linklog.facundopri.com/privacy | grep '<title>'` shows the policy title.
   - Module system: the page is an app asset; the landing only links to it. No new Worker, route or dependency.
2. **Rejected build variant:** a second Bun HTML entrypoint. It adds an empty script chunk, a source map and a `<script type="module">` to a page that needs none (both debaters measured it).
3. **Limited Use statement:** Google's example sentence verbatim ("The use of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements."), followed by one sentence applying it to what the extension receives through Chrome's APIs. The policy page asks for "an affirmative statement that your use of the data complies with the Limited Use restrictions" and gives that sentence as its example; keeping it verbatim satisfies the rule and any literal check.
4. **Every recipient is named** (Cloudflare, TypeSafe, the saved sites; and what the browser itself contacts: Google Fonts, GitHub, YouTube, sites' images). The store policy asks to disclose "all parties" data is shared with, and Connect and the Privacy tab must say the same thing.
5. **Retention is stated as it is today**, including the indefinite tab receipts and the D1 backup window; changes become follow-ups that edit the policy when they ship.
6. **Links to the policy:** the sign-in/sign-up screen (with the consent line), the landing footer, and Connect (via FAC-305). The dashboard's privacy-policy field gets the same URL.
7. **Language:** English (the app, extension and listing are English); the AAIP notice stays in Spanish as Res. 14/2018 prescribes it. A Spanish version is **[LAWYER]** before Public.
8. **Slack:** the owner-approved paragraph (FAC-307 comment, 2026-11-25) is added in the PR that ships FAC-321, not now.
9. **The extension's "only to the Linklog page" sentence holds only once FAC-306 ships** (the packaged ZIP still bridges to localhost and 127.0.0.1): FAC-305 and FAC-306 go out in one extension release, before the policy is published.

## Owner questions

1. **Q1 — Privacy contact email.** The policy needs an address that answers requests within the legal deadlines. Options: (a) the personal Gmail already verified as the CWS contact; (b) an alias such as `privacy@linklog.facundopri.com` via Cloudflare Email Routing (needs owner setup on the zone). **Recommend (a) now**: it is already shown on the store item, works today and adds no setup; switch to (b) before Public.
2. **Q2 — Identity and domicile published.** Ley 25.326 art. 6 asks for the controller's identity and domicile. Options: (a) full name + "Ciudad Autónoma de Buenos Aires, Argentina"; (b) full name + full postal address; (c) publisher name "LinkLog" only. **Recommend (a)**: it identifies a natural person without publishing a home address; (c) likely does not meet art. 6. **[LAWYER]** whether (a) is enough.
3. **Q3 — Beta in the EU/UK.** Options: (a) exclude EEA, UK and Switzerland in the store's Distribution tab and share the Unlisted link only with testers outside them; (b) allow them and accept GDPR duties now (rights, possibly an art. 27 representative). **Recommend (a)**: a checkbox, no code, reversible; it keeps GDPR a Public-launch question. The web sign-up stays open either way.
4. **Q4 — Tab evaluation receipts.** After a lens evaluates a tab, its URL, title, description, site name, page facts and the answer stay until the account is deleted (Activity shows them). Options: (a) publish that as is, and open a follow-up to delete them after 7 days; (b) promise 7 days now and block the submission on that code. **Recommend (a)**: true today, no scope added; the policy row shortens when the follow-up ships.
5. **Q5 — Account deletion promise.** Options: (a) on request by email, within 5 business days (the art. 16 maximum), done with the admin script; (b) in-app delete before the beta. **Recommend (a)**: the law's deadline, one script, no new UI.
6. **Q6 — Terms of service now?** **Recommend no** for Unlisted and a free small beta; write them with FAC-176 before Public or charging.
7. **Q7 — AAIP database registration (Ley 25.326 art. 24).** Options: (a) ask a lawyer and register before Public; (b) register before the beta. **Recommend (a)**: it does not block the store submission, the procedure is No verificado, and a small beta is the time to get the lawyer's read on whether Linklog is in scope.

### Linklog Privacy Policy

Last updated: [OWNER: publication date]

This policy covers the Linklog web app at linklog.facundopri.com, its upload API, and the Linklog extension for Chrome. It says what data Linklog handles, where it goes, how long it is kept, and how you can see, fix or delete it.

#### Who is responsible

Linklog is run by [OWNER Q2: full legal name], an individual based in Ciudad Autónoma de Buenos Aires, Argentina. That person is responsible for your data (the "responsable" under Argentine Law 25.326). Contact: [OWNER Q1: contact email]. Linklog is in beta and is not a company.

#### What Linklog does

Linklog is a link library. You save links, Linklog fetches and extracts their content, and AI tags and lenses help you sort them. With the extension, Linklog's Browser view shows the tabs of the Chrome window where Linklog is open, so you can review, group, evaluate and save them.

#### Data we handle and why

**Your account.** Email address, name, a password hash (if you use a password), whether your email is verified, and account dates. We use them to sign you in and to send sign-in, verification and password-reset emails. Linklog sends no newsletters or marketing email.

**Sign-in sessions.** Each session records the IP address and browser user agent it was created from. A session ends after 7 days without use; using Linklog extends it. To limit abuse, Linklog counts sign-in and account requests per IP address (kept up to 24 hours) and writes, new links and sign-in emails per account (kept up to 2 days; the email counter stores only a hash of the address).

**Your library.** The links you save or send through the upload API, their titles, descriptions, preview images and favicons, the extracted content (page text, YouTube transcripts, text read from images in social media posts), your tags and lenses with their questions, and the AI answers about your links.

**API keys.** If you create one, we keep a hashed copy, its first nine characters (so you can recognise it), its name and when it was used.

**Activity and usage records.** For each job (fetching a link, extracting content, an AI evaluation) we keep its status, errors, the provider and model used, token counts and an estimated cost, to show you Activity and Usage and to run and fix the service.

**Data from the extension.** [OWNER: after FAC-305 ships: "The extension reads no tab data until you accept this in Connect."] After you accept, the extension reads, in the Chrome window where Linklog is open:

- the URL, title, favicon and group of each tab, through Chrome's `tabs` and `tabGroups` permissions;
- if you allow page content (Chrome's optional access to all sites), a preview of each page (description, image, site name) and its text as already loaded in the tab.

The extension never reads cookies, passwords, form fields or your browsing history, and never scrolls pages. When you ask it to load the content of tabs Chrome has put to sleep, it reloads those tabs and puts them back to sleep.

The extension passes this data only to the Linklog page open in the same window; it sends nothing to any server itself. From that page, data reaches Linklog's servers in three cases only:

1. **Evaluating tabs with a lens** (or auto-grouping tabs) sends, for each tab, its URL, title, page description, site name, short page facts and page text (up to 160,000 characters) to Linklog, which passes up to about 24,000 characters of it to the AI provider below. While a lens is on in Browser view, it evaluates every open tab of that window it can read, including pages you are signed in to, and new tabs as they open. Linklog has no list of sites it skips: turn the lens off, or don't allow page content, to stop it.
2. **Saving a tab** sends only its URL. Your library then fetches the page itself, like any link you add.
3. **Pinned groups** send the group's name, description and color, never its tabs.

Listing, grouping, moving and closing tabs stays in your browser. The extension keeps settings and short-lived results in Chrome's extension storage on your device.

#### Where data goes

Linklog does not sell your data, does not use it for advertising, and does not share it with data brokers. It goes only to these providers, and only to run Linklog:

| Recipient | What it receives | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cloudflare, Inc.** (United States, global network) | All the data above: hosting (Workers), database (D1), file storage (R2), queues and workflows; Browser Rendering, which opens the pages you save to read them; Workers AI, which reads text in images of saved posts; email delivery for sign-in emails; DNS lookups of the sites you save; error logs and traces | Running Linklog |
| **TypeSafe** (typesafe.ai, United States) | For each AI tag or lens evaluation: the link's or tab's URL, title, site, description and text (up to about 24,000 characters), and your tag or lens questions (for auto-grouping, the names and descriptions of the groups you chose) | Answering your tags and lenses. TypeSafe states that it does not train models on this input. |
| **The websites you save** (including YouTube, X and Instagram) | A request from Linklog's servers (not from your IP) for the page, video page, transcript or media you saved | Fetching the content you asked for |

While you use the web app, your browser also connects directly to **Google Fonts** (the app's font; it sees your IP address), **GitHub** (to check the current extension version), **YouTube** (video thumbnails, and its embedded player when you open a saved video) and **the sites in your library and tabs** (their own favicons and preview images). Those requests follow those services' own policies.

We may also disclose data when Argentine law or a court order requires it, or to protect users and the service against fraud or abuse.

#### International transfers

Linklog's providers store and process data outside Argentina, mainly in the United States, which Argentina's data protection authority does not list as a country with adequate protection. When you create an account or sign in for the first time you consent to this transfer, which is needed to provide the service. #### How long we keep data

| Data | Kept |
| --- | --- |
| Account, library, tags, lenses, API keys | Until you delete them or your account is deleted |
| A link you delete | Its database rows and stored content are deleted promptly. Its title can stay in Activity and Usage until the next 5-minute cleanup after its batch finishes; then they show "Deleted link". Usage keeps provider, model and token counts without the link. |
| Page text sent for a tab evaluation | Deleted as soon as the evaluation finishes; a cleanup every 5 minutes removes any left behind, and unconfirmed ones after 7 days |
| Tab results the extension never confirmed | 7 days |
| URL, title, description, site name and up to 20 short page facts of each tab you evaluated, and the lens answer | Until your account is deleted [OWNER Q4] |
| Sessions | 7 days after last use |
| Abuse-limit counters | Up to 24 hours (per IP) and 2 days (per account) |
| Server logs and traces (without query strings) | Up to 7 days [OWNER: confirm Cloudflare plan] |
| Database backups | Deleted data can stay restorable for up to 30 days |

#### Your rights

You can ask us to tell you what data we hold about you (we answer within 10 calendar days; access is free at intervals of six months or more, unless you show a legitimate interest), to correct or update it, or to delete it, including your whole account and its stored content (within 5 business days). You can delete links, tags and lenses yourself in the app. Account deletion is not in the app yet: email [OWNER Q1: contact email] from your account's address and we will delete it [OWNER Q5]. We may need to confirm the request comes from you.

#### Chrome Web Store Limited Use

The use of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. This covers what the Linklog extension receives through Chrome's extension APIs: tab URLs, titles and page content. Linklog uses that data only to provide the Browser view features you use, transfers it only to the providers listed above to provide those features, and never sells it, uses it for advertising or uses it to determine creditworthiness. No person reads it, except with your explicit consent for specific data (for example a support request), to investigate abuse or protect security, to comply with the law, or in aggregated, anonymized form for internal operations.

#### Security

All traffic between your browser, the extension's page and Linklog uses HTTPS. Passwords and API keys are stored only as hashes.

#### Cookies and local storage

Linklog sets only its own sign-in cookies (names starting with `linklog.`), needed to keep you signed in, and keeps interface preferences in your browser's local storage. Linklog has no analytics, no ads and no tracking cookies.

#### Changes

We will update this page when Linklog's data practices change, and change the date at the top. If a change gives Linklog new uses for data you already gave us, or makes the extension read new kinds of data, we will tell you by email or in the app before it applies.
